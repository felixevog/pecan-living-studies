# PECAN Living Study Overview

This repository provides a **continuously updated overview of studies using Perceived Causal Networks (PECAN)**. It accompanies the PECAN Guidelines and replaces the previously maintained living version hosted on the Open Science Framework (OSF).

The purpose of this page is to keep the study overview current as new PECAN studies are published, released as preprints, or otherwise become publicly available.

## How to cite this resource

Please cite the PECAN Guidelines rather than this repository:

**Vogel et al. (2026). _How perceived causal networks can complement case conceptualization, diagnostic classification, and data-based networks._**

## Empirical studies using PECAN

| Study | Objective | Population | Administration | Node selection | Edge assessment | Main analyses / metrics | Main findings |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Klintwall, Bellander, & Cervin (2023).** *Perceived Causal Problem Networks: Reliability, Central Problems, and Clinical Utility for Depression* | Introduce PECAN and examine feasibility, reliability, central problems, and clinical utility | N = 231 adults screening positive for depression; n = 50 therapists | Unguided online assessment | Participants selected relevant problems from 26 predefined psychiatric problems | For each problem, participants selected up to three perceived causes and distributed causal influence to sum to 100% | Weighted out-degree centrality; immediate test–retest reliability | PECAN was feasible and therapists generally considered the networks clinically useful. Centrality showed high immediate reliability, whereas individual edges were less reliable. |
| **Andreasson et al. (2023).** *Perceived Causal Networks: Clinical Utility Evaluated by Therapists and Patients* | Evaluate PECAN as a case-conceptualization tool at the beginning of psychotherapy | N = 5 adult patients screening positive for depression and their therapists | Unguided online assessment with interactive network visualization | 26 predefined problems with free-text specification | Original PECAN causal assessment | Patient and therapist ratings of clinical utility and validity | Networks were highly individualized and were generally perceived as useful by both patients and therapists. The study highlighted the potential value of adding contextual factors. |
| **Bångstad et al. (2024).** *Perceived causal symptom network of adolescent mental health issues* | Examine reliability of individual and aggregated PECAN networks and characterize adolescent mental-health networks | N = 55 adolescents aged 16–19 screening positive for depression | Unguided online assessment; repeated after two weeks | Transdiagnostic predefined item list with individual specification | Perceived causal relations among selected problems | Test–retest reliability; out-degree centrality; feedback loops; aggregated network | Individual networks showed low average retest reliability, whereas the aggregated network was substantially more stable. Procrastination and overthinking were particularly central, and several recurring feedback loops were identified. |
| **Burger et al. (2024).** *A novel approach for constructing personalized networks from longitudinal perceived causal relations* | Introduce **Longitudinal PECAN (L-PECAN)** to assess perceived causal relations repeatedly over time | N = 20 adults screening positive for depression | Brief daily assessments across four weeks | Personalized set of relevant symptoms | Repeated assessment of perceived symptom interactions over time | Network stability, required observations, node inclusion, structural change | Approximately 40% of participants achieved stable personalized networks; relatively few showed substantial changes in network structure. |
| **Kaariniemi et al. (2025).** *Perceived causal networks created using structured interviews: feasibility and reliability* | Evaluate feasibility, acceptability, and reliability of interviewer-guided PECAN | N = 21 adolescents aged 15–18 with self-defined mental-health problems | Two structured online interviews within one week | 17 predefined items; up to 10 prioritized based on frequency × severity; free-text specification and option for a self-defined problem | Pairwise ratings of how often X leads to Y | Test–retest of causal links and centrality | Mean reliability was rₛ = .53 for causal links and .70 for centrality. Most adolescents found the interview easy to understand. |
| **Schenström et al. (2025).** *The Sunny Side of the Network Approach to Psychopathology: Comparing Nodes as Either Problems or Strengths* | Compare pathogenic PECAN formulations with equivalent salutogenic/strength-based formulations | N = 9 adolescents and n = 20 therapists | Structured interviews using both problem- and strength-focused formulations | Equivalent domains framed once as problems and once as strengths | Causal relations assessed within each formulation | Participant and therapist ratings of usefulness and informativeness | Pathogenic and salutogenic networks were perceived as complementary, demonstrating that perceived causal networks can be formulated around both difficulties and strengths. |
| **Scholten et al. (2025).** *Updating Patient Perceptions With Intensive Longitudinal Data for Enhanced Case Conceptualizations: An Approach With Bayesian Informative Priors* | Combine patient-perceived causal networks with intensive longitudinal data | N = 12 psychotherapy patients, primarily treated for depression, and their therapists | PECAN followed by EMA six times daily for 15 days | Personalized symptoms identified for the individual PECAN and longitudinal assessment | PECAN relations used as prior information and updated with longitudinal data | Comparison of PECAN prior networks, data networks, and Bayesian posterior networks; face validity | Bayesian posterior networks received the highest face-validity ratings. PECAN, data-derived, and posterior networks nevertheless showed substantial structural differences. |
| **Vogel et al. (2025).** *An Adaptation of Perceived Causal Networks for Children and Adolescents (PECAN-CA): An Evaluation of its Reliability and Feasibility* | Adapt PECAN for children and adolescents and evaluate feasibility and reliability | N = 75 participants aged 10–21 with elevated social-anxiety symptoms | Face-to-face semi-structured **PECAN-CA** interview with psychoeducation | Personalized selection of symptoms from a social-anxiety-informed pool, with options for individual symptoms | Pairwise perceived causal relations among selected symptoms | Immediate and four-week retest of symptoms, severity, causal relations, and centrality | PECAN-CA was feasible and showed particularly high reliability for centrality measures (r = .89–.95). Reliability improved with age, understanding, and fewer selected symptoms. |
| **Vogel et al. (2026).** *Heterogeneity of Symptomatology in Socially Anxious Adolescents: Are Case Conceptualizations Based on Perceived Causal Networks (PECAN) Able to Capture Personalized Symptoms and their Relations?* | Examine heterogeneity in symptoms and perceived causal structures in socially anxious youth | Socially anxious children and adolescents | Interviewer-guided PECAN-CA | Personalized selection and specification of social-anxiety-relevant symptoms | Pairwise perceived causal relations | Individual and aggregated symptom/network characteristics | The study illustrates substantial heterogeneity in symptom composition and perceived causal relations that is not represented by a single generic disorder model. |
| **Seewald et al. (2025).** *Networks for treatment selection in psychotherapy: providing a manual for process-based perceived causal networks* | Develop and evaluate a practical process-based perceived causal network procedure for treatment selection | N = 12 psychotherapy patients; n = 8 therapists | Collaborative therapist–patient paper-and-pencil procedure | Individual maladaptive processes surrounding a primary problem | Perceived causal relations among maintaining processes | Feasibility, visualization quality, clinical utility, and changes in common therapeutic factors | Patients and therapists rated the procedure as feasible and clinically useful. This represents a broader **process-based extension of the perceived causal network approach** rather than standard symptom-based PECAN. |
| **Irnstorfer, Baumann, & Vogel (2026).** *Exploring the Impact of Perceived Causal Symptom Networks on Adolescents’ Experience and Coping with Depression: A Qualitative Study* — **Preprint** | Explore whether collaboratively constructing a PECAN-CA network influences adolescents’ understanding, coping, and everyday behavior | N = 10 adolescents aged 14–17 meeting DSM-5 criteria for Major Depressive Disorder | Structured collaborative PECAN-CA session; follow-up interview after two weeks | Individually selected depressive and related symptoms | Individual perceived causal relations among selected symptoms | Inductive qualitative content analysis; supportive pre–post symptom and illness-perception measures | Adolescents reported greater awareness of symptom interactions, reflection on maintaining cycles, and easier communication about depression. Several described deliberate attempts to change symptoms or behaviors based on their network. |
| **Klintwall et al. (2026).** *Parental Perceived Causal Networks of Problematic Screen Time in Adolescents: The Centrality of Passive Scrolling* | Examine parents’ perceived causal models of adolescent screen-related and mental-health problems | N = 128 parents reporting on adolescents aged 12–19 | Unguided online assessment | Participants selected relevant problems from 20 predefined child-, screen-, and parent-related domains | Participants rated perceived causal relations; causal-question framing was experimentally varied | Out-degree centrality; reliability and group comparisons | Passive scrolling emerged as the problem most frequently perceived to influence other difficulties. Parental behaviors were relatively rarely perceived as causal. |
| **Bridger, Maltby, Nettle, Fried, & Klintwall (2026).** *Unpicking the Entanglement: A Perceived Causal Network of Biopsychosocial Factors for Mental Health* — **Preprint** | Examine how a UK-representative public sample organizes perceived causal relations among biological, psychological, and social factors linked to mental health | N = 509 UK adults, representative by age, gender, and ethnicity; an independent n = 120 corroborated domain classifications | Unguided online assessment | 20 predefined biopsychosocial factors (2 biological, 7 psychological, 11 social) | Each participant rated a random subset of 50 directed causal relations from the possible network | Mean directed edge ratings; within- vs between-domain relations; centrality; subgroup comparisons | Psychological factors were perceived more strongly as effects than causes and as influenced by both biological and social factors. Between-domain causal influences were stronger than within-domain influences. |
| **Reichert et al. (2026).** *Perceived Causal Networks in Patients with Fibromyalgia and Depression: Construction of a Structured Interview and Testing Reliability* | Evaluate structured PECAN interviews in diagnostically confirmed clinical populations | N = 100 adults: n = 50 with MDD and n = 50 with fibromyalgia | Two semi-structured interviews one week apart | Participants selected 10 personally relevant symptoms from a broad predefined symptom list | Ratings of perceived causal influence between selected symptoms | Test–retest reliability of edge weights and centrality; feasibility; qualitative analysis of inconsistencies | Mean test–retest reliability was r = .62 for edge weights and r = .76 for out-degree centrality. Participants generally rated PECAN as comprehensible and manageable. |
| **Lavefjord et al. (2026).** *Testing the network centrality hypothesis within process-based acceptance and commitment therapy – A single case experiment utilizing perceived causal networks* | Experimentally test whether targeting the most central PECAN process produces greater improvement than targeting the least central process | Six participants in a chronic-pain/process-based ACT context | PECAN-informed single-case experimental treatment design | Psychological-inflexibility processes | Perceived causal relations used to identify more vs less central treatment targets | Randomized within-person comparison; pain interference, motivation, and pain intensity | All participants showed at least one outcome changing in the hypothesized direction, although effects were not uniformly consistent. The study provides a direct experimental test of PECAN-guided treatment selection. |

## Methods and resources

**Vogel et al. (2026). _How perceived causal networks can complement case conceptualization, diagnostic classification, and data-based networks._**  
The **PECAN Guidelines** and the primary citation for this Living Study Overview.

**Reichert et al. (2026). _Analyzing Perceived Causal Networks with PECAN2: A Practical R Tutorial._**  
Practical tutorial for importing, processing, analysing, summarizing, and visualizing PECAN data using the **PECAN2 R package**.

**Gauld, Blanken, Klintwall, & Micoulaud-Franchi (2025). _Introducing Perceived Causal Networks in Sleep Medicine._ Journal of Sleep Research, 34(6), e70035.**  
Conceptual application of PECAN to sleep medicine, outlining how perceived causal networks may complement conventional diagnostic classification and support personalized assessment in sleep disorders. [https://doi.org/10.1111/jsr.70035](https://doi.org/10.1111/jsr.70035)

## Related methodological developments

**Vogel et al. (2026). _Assessing Person-Specific Symptom Networks From Brief Narratives: Development and Initial Evaluation of the Narrative-to-Network (N2N) Approach._**  
N2N derives directed person-specific symptom networks from brief prompted spoken narratives. PECAN serves as a structured comparison method in the initial evaluation; N2N is therefore listed here as a closely related methodological development rather than as a PECAN study.

## Scope

The overview includes publicly available empirical studies using perceived causal network methods at the individual or group level, as well as selected methodological and conceptual extensions directly related to PECAN. Depending on the study, PECAN may be used for assessment, case conceptualization, comparison with other network approaches, prediction, treatment planning, methodological development, or intervention research.
